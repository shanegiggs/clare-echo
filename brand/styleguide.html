Section 0

Using this guide

The Echo has one job on the page: get a Clare reader to the story fast, and look like nobody else in the county doing it. Every rule here serves that. Where a rule would make a story harder to read, the story wins — then tell us, so the rule can be fixed.

0.1

What is fixed and what is yours

Three things are fixed and never improvised: the wordmark, the two brand colours, and Inter. Everything else — crops, column counts, how bold a splash runs — is a judgement call for whoever is on the page that day.

Confirm
Measurements marked “confirm” are proposals, not press facts. Trim sizes, advert dimensions and Pantone matches in this guide were set from the existing paper and the website. Check them against your printer’s current spec sheet and a physical Pantone guide before they go on a contract proof.

Section 1

The logo

The Clare Echo logo is a drawn mark supplied as vector artwork. It is never re-typed, re-traced or rebuilt — always place the master file. There are two lockups and four colourways, and between them they cover every job.

1.1

Two lockups

Use the horizontal lockup by default. Reach for the stacked lockup only when the space is squarer than it is wide — a footer column, a social avatar, a stamp on merchandise, the corner of a stand.

HorizontalDefault. Ratio 5.16 : 1.
StackedConstrained widths. Ratio 1.93 : 1.

1.2

The arcs

The three arcs breaking off the “o” are part of the mark, not an optional flourish. They are the echo. Never delete them, never redraw them, never animate them, and never use them on their own as a standalone icon.

Colour
The arcs carry Echo Blue whenever the wordmark is black, and go white with the wordmark in the knockout version. There is no black-arcs version and no blue-wordmark-with-blue-arcs version other than the partner lock-up in 1.4.

1.3

Clear space and minimum size

Keep clear space equal to the cap height of the “C” on all four sides, measured from the outermost edge of the artwork — which on the horizontal lockup is the tip of the longest arc, not the “o”. Nothing sits inside it: not a strapline, not a page number, not the edge of a photograph.

Section 3

Typography

Inter, throughout. It is licensed under the SIL Open Font License, which means it can be installed on every machine in the office, embedded in a PDF and sent to a printer with no licence to buy and no per-seat count to track.

3.2

Print type scale

Everything below headline size is set solid — leading equal to point size, 8 on 8, 12 on 12. It is a tight, deliberate colour on the page and it is the house setting; do not open it up per story. Headlines are the exception and take proportional leading.

Tracking is given in InDesign units — thousandths of an em — which map one to one onto the website’s CSS values. Inter is drawn tight, so it needs negative tracking as it gets large and positive tracking as it gets small.

Section 4

Grid & formats

Editorial runs on a seven-column grid — every story, picture and nib is built from whole columns. Display advertising is sold separately, on fractions of the page width rather than on those columns.

4.1

The newspaper page

Two grids, and they do not line up — by design. Editorial is seven columns; display advertising is sold on fractions of the page width, so a half page is 128 mm (half the 265 mm width, less the centre gutter) rather than a whole number of columns. Do not try to force an advert onto the editorial columns, or an editorial leg onto an advert width.

The live area is taken from the full-page advert, so it is solid. Confirm the column gutter, the trim and the head/foot margins with the press before building master pages — the column width above follows from the gutter.

Setting ads
An advert is the one place on the page where the Echo’s own rules do not apply — it is the advertiser’s brand, not ours. What the Echo does insist on: a 3 mm keyline or a clear tint edge on any advert with a white background, so it never reads as editorial, and no use of the Echo wordmark inside advertiser artwork without sign-off.

Section 5

Photography

The Echo’s pictures are the reason people pick it up. They are taken in Clare, by people who were standing there. Nothing here should ever be true of a stock library.

5.1

What an Echo picture looks like

People, named Available light Shot at the event, not staged after Faces readable at 40 mm A recognisable Clare place in frame where possible Colour as it was, not graded

Avoid: heavy filters, tilted horizons, vignettes, borders, drop shadows on cut-outs, and any AI-generated or composited image in a news context — without exception.

5.2

Captions and credits

Every picture is captioned and every photographer is credited. The caption is a sentence; the credit is small caps in Slate and sits at the end of it.

Quilty village, west Clare, where councillors have called for new rural housing guidelines. Photograph: Ann O’Connell

Rule
The credit belongs under the picture, never inside the story. Caption text that leaks into the opening paragraph is the single most common fault in the current archive — it makes the story read as though it begins mid-sentence.

7.1

Rules that apply to every template

  • The wordmark sits bottom-left, always. Readers should find it without looking. It never moves to a corner because the picture is awkward — re-crop the picture.
  • One headline, no standfirst. A social card carries the headline and nothing else. If it needs explaining, it needs a different picture.
  • Type never sits on a busy part of a photograph. Use the ink band. Do not drop a shadow behind the words to make it work.
  • Credit the photographer on social too. Bottom-right, or in the first line of the caption.

Section 8

House style

The written conventions that make an Echo story recognisable before you reach the byline. These are already how the paper writes — they are recorded here so they survive a change of staff.

8.1

The opening

Every story opens with its place or subject in caps, running straight into the sentence. It is the Echo’s signature and it should not be dropped.

KILMALEY have been one of the standout teams in the Clare SHC but are well aware qualification is not guaranteed.

WEST Clare councillors have called for new national rural housing guidelines to be brought into effect as quickly as possible.

A SIXMILEBRIDGE gym founded by first cousins is continuing to grow as the village’s population expands.

Typesetting
Inter carries every fada, so there is no excuse for a stripped accent in print or on screen. If one drops out, the text was pasted through a system that lost the encoding — re-key it rather than leaving it.

Section 9

Before it prints

A last pass, whether it is a page, an advert or a banner for the county final.

  • Colour mode is CMYK, and blacks are right. K100 for text, 60/40/40/100 for solid areas. An RGB black that slipped through prints as a muddy brown.
  • Echo Blue is spot, or you have accepted the process version. Check which the job was quoted for.
  • Images are 300 dpi at final size — 200 dpi is acceptable on newsprint, nothing lower.
  • Fonts embedded, PDF/X-1a. Inter’s licence permits embedding, so there is nothing to clear.
  • Wordmark has its clear space and is at or above minimum size.
  • Every photograph is captioned and credited.
  • Fadas are intact in every headline, byline and caption.
  • No body copy reversed out of a solid at less than 10pt.
  • Bleed is 3 mm and nothing important sits within 5 mm of trim.
